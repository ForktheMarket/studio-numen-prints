This harbor city print shows a cluster of skyscrapers reflected upside down in still water, so the city and its mirror image meet in the middle of the picture, made for living rooms, offices and apartments.

The towers are painted in loose vertical strokes of charcoal, slate blue and silver gray, with bands of warm amber windows. Below the waterline the same shapes repeat in softer, longer streaks, blurring into the water. The sky is pale misty blue, and the whole scene feels half real, half dissolved.

The palette is cool blue gray with a glow of gold, calm and slightly abstract, which suits modern interiors and gallery walls. It works above a sofa, behind a desk or in a hallway, and belongs to anyone who likes cityscapes that lean toward abstract painting. It also sits comfortably next to the blue hour skyline print as a pair above a long sofa.
